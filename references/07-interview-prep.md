# Stage 5b — Prepare for assessments and interviews

Goal: convert an invitation into a prepared candidate. This stage is where a strong resume pipeline
turns into offers.

## First: classify what you have been invited to

The invitation text tells you which of these you face, and each needs different prep:

| Signal | What it is | Prep focus |
|---|---|---|
| "online assessment", "测评", "笔试" | Aptitude / psychometric / coding screen | Format, timing, practice sets |
| "心理测评", "性格测试" | Personality / integrity screen | Consistency, role fit — **not** "correct answers" |
| "AI interview", "视频面", one-way link | Recorded video interview | Structure, delivery, time limits |
| "Manager interview", "业务面" | Hiring-manager interview | Resume depth, role fit, structured thinking |
| "HR interview", "HR面" | HR screen | Motivation, logistics, stability, salary |
| "Case interview" | Consulting-style case | Frameworks, structure, mental maths |

Record the invitation details immediately: date/time in the correct time zone, platform, dress code,
camera requirement, and any link or dial-in.

## Personality / psychometric assessments

These are the most misunderstood. There is usually **no way to "get it wrong"**, but there is a way to
look careless:

- **Consistency is the main thing measured.** Items repeat in reworded form to detect random or
  contradictory responding. Decide on a stable self-presentation *before* starting and keep to it.
- **Do not over-idealise.** Instruments include validity scales; a flawless saint profile reads as
  inauthentic. Owning ordinary human traits is stronger.
- **Answer in role terms.** For a customer-facing or marketing role, a broadly outgoing, proactive,
  resilient profile fits; for a deep-analytical role, a methodical, detail-driven profile fits.
- **Avoid extreme negative items** and avoid answering everything identically.
- **Do it in one sitting**, in a quiet setting, with time to finish. Interruption drives inconsistency.
- **Know the dimensions** commonly measured: conscientiousness, emotional stability, resilience under
  pressure, teamwork, integrity, motivation/stability, and values fit.

Note for engineering-side roles: some employers also run **game-based** assessments (rapid
perception/decision games). These measure reaction and risk appetite; there is nothing to study, only
a stable internet connection and a distraction-free environment.

## Recorded video interviews

- Rehearse a small library of stories, not scripts — the same STAR stories, retold to fit.
- Time-box every answer: aim for the middle of the allowed window.
- Look at the camera, not the screen; check lighting and framing once, then leave it.
- Have a one-page "story sheet" beside the screen so you never blank on a project detail.

## Manager (business) interviews

The core insight: **most of the interview is your own resume.** The interviewer has already decided
your background is plausible; they are now testing depth, honesty, and fit.

Prepare, per resume line, in three layers:

1. **STAR** — Situation, Task, Action, Result, plus what you learned.
2. **The follow-up** — for each project, the two questions a sceptical expert would ask (the hardest
   problem; the number you claim). Have the answers ready.
3. **The transfer** — one sentence on how that experience maps onto *this* role's requirements.

Then prepare:

- **Why this role / this employer** — say something specific and verifiable about the employer
  (a practice, a recent initiative, a product), not flattery.
- **Strengths and a genuine weakness**, with the mitigation.
- **A pressure or failure story** with the adjustment and the lesson.
- **Your questions for them** — two or three that show you have thought about the work.

For AI/technology-adjacent roles, be precise about your own level:

- Do not claim to build what you have only used. "I build with these tools" and "I understand the
  concepts behind them" are different claims; make the one that is true.
- Expect concept questions — for AI roles, a working explanation of large language models, prompting,
  retrieval-augmented generation, fine-tuning versus prompting, and what an agent is and how it
  differs from a plain chatbot.
- Expect tool questions — be able to describe what you actually did with each tool you list.

For data/analytics roles, be ready to **discuss SQL aloud**: filtering, joins, grouping and
aggregation, `WHERE` versus `HAVING`, `INNER` versus `LEFT` join, `UNION` versus `UNION ALL`. Practise
explaining your approach before writing it.

For consulting-style roles, rehearse **one framework** cold: restate the question, structure the
problem (MECE), hypothesise, identify what data would confirm or kill the hypothesis, then recommend.
Practise on a simple profitability or growth case out loud.

## The honesty rule, applied to interviews

Everything you said in the resume, you must be able to defend in the interview. This is why the
tailoring stage refuses to fabricate: an invented bullet becomes an unanswerable question. If you are
caught short, the correct move is to say what you *do* know and how you would find out — not to
improvise a fact.

## The day before

- [ ] Time zone, platform, and link tested end to end.
- [ ] Camera, microphone, lighting, background, and a quiet room arranged.
- [ ] Dress code met; camera-on requirement respected.
- [ ] The submitted resume and the JD snapshot open on a second screen or on paper.
- [ ] The story sheet, plus pen and paper for case work.
- [ ] Two or three questions for the interviewer written down.
- [ ] Phone silenced; notifications off.

## The 24 hours after

- Write down every question asked while it is fresh.
- Write down what you would answer differently.
- Update the tracker with the outcome and the next expected step.
- Feed anything you got wrong back into your story sheet.
