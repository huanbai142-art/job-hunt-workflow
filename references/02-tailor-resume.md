# Stage 2 — Tailor the resume to the JD

This is the heart of the workflow. The goal is **not** to make a resume that contains the JD's
keywords. The goal is to make a resume where **every JD requirement is answered by concrete
evidence**, written in the JD's own language.

## The failure mode to avoid

The weak pattern, seen constantly in student resumes, is *experience stacking*:

> - Used Excel to record and summarize business data, improving data-collection ability.
> - Participated in a research project and completed data analysis.
> - Responsible for event coordination, exercising communication skills.

Every line is a job title with a verb bolted on. Nothing answers a requirement. The reader learns
what the candidate *was near*, not what they *did* or *achieved*. This reads as low effort even
when the underlying experience is strong.

The fix is a mapping discipline, covered below.

## Method: JD requirement → evidence → bullet

### Step 1 — Decompose the JD

Turn the posting into an explicit, numbered list of requirement units. Keep the employer's wording.

| # | Requirement (verbatim or near-verbatim) | Type |
|---|---|---|
| R1 | Own overseas-market forecast collection, submission, and variance analysis | Responsibility |
| R2 | Familiar with manufacturing supply-chain operations | Must-have |
| R3 | Cross-departmental coordination and risk control | Responsibility |
| R4 | Student-organization / campus-marketing experience (leader preferred) | Nice-to-have |

### Step 2 — Build the evidence bank

List everything the candidate actually did, at the level of **actions and outcomes**, not titles.
One line per piece of evidence, with the hard facts attached (numbers, scale, tools, results).

If the candidate has an existing "master resume", mine it — but strip it back to facts first.

### Step 3 — Map one requirement to one piece of evidence

For each requirement, choose the **single strongest** piece of evidence. Write it down as a mapping.

| # | Requirement | Chosen evidence | Gap? |
|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | Forecast collection + variance analysis | Ran a multi-party pilot: tracked progress, collected forecasts, analysed variance | No |
| R2 | Manufacturing supply chain | Coursework in managerial economics incl. supply-chain analysis | Partial — theory only |
| R3 | Cross-departmental coordination | Coordinated government / platform / co-op stakeholders in the pilot | No |
| R4 | Campus marketing / leadership | Led a 50+ applicant recruitment drive end-to-end | No |

Requirements with no evidence at all become an honest gap. Cover a partial gap with the closest
real thing and let the reader judge — never upgrade coursework into a job.

### Step 4 — Rewrite each bullet in the JD's language

Now write. Each bullet should:

- open with an **action verb** that mirrors the JD's verb,
- carry the **specifics** (who, what scale, which tool, what result),
- close with the **transferable skill** named in the JD's vocabulary,
- be **2–3 lines**, not one short clause.

The bullet should make the reader think *"that is exactly R1"* without ever seeing the letters R1.

### Step 5 — Summarize

The 3–4 line summary at the top, and the keyword line under the name, should be assembled from the
requirement units the candidate *does* match. Nothing from the units they do not.

## Worked example (de-identified)

**JD requirement (R):**
> Support online/offline marketing channels: prepare channel materials, follow up on stocking, and
> collect and summarize sales data to keep channel operations on schedule.

**Weak bullet (before):**
> Participated in event planning and execution, improving communication skills.

**Strong bullet (after):**
> Supported end-to-end event delivery: wrote copy, produced promotional assets, coordinated guest and
> participant outreach, and compiled registration data for review — keeping the event on schedule and
> reaching the target student segment, which built hands-on channel-support and data-collection skills.

**Second requirement (R'):**
> New-product marketing: support launch planning and execution, including copywriting, material
> preparation, and supplier coordination.

**Strong bullet (after):**
> Co-owned the launch pipeline from planning to delivery: drafted promotional copy, prepared materials,
> and coordinated suppliers, then collected feedback and ran a retrospective — forming a
> plan → execute → collect data → improve loop.

Note what changed: the requirement's own nouns (channel materials, stocking follow-up, sales data,
on schedule) reappear, the scale and tools are concrete, and the "learned skill" sentence names the
JD's competence instead of a generic one.

## STAR / CAR shaping

Each bullet is a compressed STAR or CAR:

- **STAR** — Situation, Task, Action, Result.
- **CAR** — Challenge, Action, Result.

Compression rules:

- One bullet = one action + one result + one named skill.
- The Result does not have to be a number. "Delivered on schedule", "adopted by the team",
  "shortlisted", "ranked top 20%" are results.
- Prefer **real** numbers. If a number is unknown or unverifiable, describe the scale qualitatively
  rather than inventing one.

## Honesty rules

- No invented metrics, titles, dates, or employers.
- No upgrading coursework into employment, or a class project into a client engagement.
- No claiming a tool was "used" if the candidate only read about it. Write "coursework in X",
  "currently learning X", or "familiar with the concepts of X".
- Date ranges must stay consistent across every tailored version. Track them once in the profile
  file and reuse.
- If the candidate asks to stretch a fact, decline and offer the honest, stronger phrasing instead.

## Professional-writer persona (use this framing)

When tailoring, adopt the posture of a **senior campus-recruiting resume writer**:

- The reader spends ~8 seconds on the first pass. Make the top third answer the JD.
- Recruiters scan for evidence of the JD's verbs, not for adjectives about the candidate.
- Mirror the employer's nouns — the same competence under their word is worth more than a synonym.
- Prefer concrete over impressive. A 3-line concrete bullet beats a grand vague one.
- Cut anything that does not serve a requirement. Length is not seriousness.
- End every bullet on capability, so the reader knows what the candidate can now do for them.

## Per-JD deliverable

For each posting, produce:

1. the requirement table,
2. the evidence mapping (including marked gaps),
3. the tailored resume config,
4. the built document(s),
5. one or two lines of notes on what was emphasized and what was left out.

Store the config so the resume can be re-generated later without redoing the analysis.
